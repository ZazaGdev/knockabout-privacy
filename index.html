Privacy Policy for Knockabout

Effective date: 8 September 2026 Last updated: 15 September 2026

Knockabout is a party game for people in the same room. It has no accounts, no sign-up, no advertising and no analytics. This policy explains the small amount of data the app does handle, why it exists, and how long it lasts.

The short version

You do not create an account and we never ask for your name, email address, phone number or any other personal detail. Games played on a single phone send nothing anywhere. Games where everyone uses their own phone send only what the game itself has to show the other players in your room, and that content is deleted when the room ends. A room ends an hour after the last thing anybody in it does, and a clean-up job removes expired rooms every few minutes. Caught is the exception: it is played across a whole evening with the phone put away between turns, so a Caught room waits eight hours rather than one before it is treated as finished.

We do not sell your data, we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers, and there is nothing in the app that tracks you across other apps or websites.

What the app stores on your device

The app keeps a small amount of information on your phone only. It is never uploaded, and it is removed when you uninstall the app.

  • A display name, if you choose to enter one. The app never asks for it. Leave it blank and you are shown as "Player 1".
  • Guest names and game settings you have chosen, so a second game starts where you left off.
  • A record of your last 20 finished sessions, holding the date, the game, the number of rounds and the final scores. You can delete this at any time from the Profile screen.
  • A paused session, so closing the app mid-game does not lose the round. It expires after one hour and is then discarded. Two games wait eight hours instead, because they are meant to run across a whole evening with the phone put down between turns: Caught, and The long game.

What leaves your device, and only when it must

Nothing leaves your device in the games played on one shared phone. Those work with no network connection at all.

When you host or join a room where each person uses their own phone, the app creates a room on our server and sends the following, so that the other players in your room can see it:

WhatWhenWhy
The display name you choseOn joining a roomSo the others know whose turn it is
An anonymous identifier for your installOn first use of a roomSo the server knows which seat is yours. It is a random value with no name, email address or account attached, and we cannot tell who you are from it. While a room is alive, the name and the content you send are stored against it, which is what keeps your seat yours
Game state such as scores, votes and guessesWhile playingSo every phone shows the same game
Photos you pick, in the photo gameOnly in that game, only the ones you pickSo the room can see the photo being guessed about. The game sends the next round's photo ahead of time so it is ready when the round opens, so up to two of yours can be on the server at once
Text you type, in the games that ask for itOnly when you type itSo the room can read your statement, answer or guess
Drawings you make, in the drawing gamesOnly in those gamesSo the room can see and guess at your drawing

We do not receive anything else. The app has no access to your contacts, your calendar, your location, your microphone or your camera, and it never reads your photo library. When you choose a photo, your phone's own picker hands us that one image and nothing more.

Photos, in more detail

Photos are the most sensitive thing the app handles, so they get the most care.

  • Before a photo leaves your phone it is re-encoded, and the app checks the resulting bytes and refuses to upload anything still carrying embedded metadata. This means location data recorded by your camera cannot reach our server, because the upload fails rather than proceeding.
  • Photos are stored in a private area that is not readable from the public internet and not listed anywhere. They are reachable only through short-lived links issued to the players in your room.
  • A photo is deleted when its round ends. Because the next round's photo is sent ahead of time, an unused one waits, unseen by anybody, until its own round ends or the game finishes. If a game is abandoned part-way, at most two images are left behind, and they are removed automatically the next time anybody starts a photo game. They stay in the same private area until then, reachable by nobody.

How long anything lasts on our server

  • A room and everything in it lasts as long as the room is in use. The hour runs from the last thing ANYBODY in the room does, not from the last thing you do, so a room you have left stays alive while the others are still playing. Once that hour has passed the room is deleted by a job that runs every five minutes, so deletion happens within about an hour and five minutes of the room going quiet. Deleting a room deletes all of its contents: names, scores, text, drawings and votes.
  • A Caught room waits eight hours, not one. That game is not played in one sitting: everybody is given something to do and the phones go away for the evening, so an hour of quiet is normal play rather than an abandoned room. The same clean-up job removes it, so deletion happens within about eight hours and five minutes of the last thing anybody in it does. Nothing else is different: the contents are the same, and they go the same way.
  • The anonymous identifier for your install is deleted after 30 days without use. There is no way to recover it, and reinstalling the app produces a new one.
  • Nothing is archived, backed up for analysis, or retained after the above. There is no history on our server for anyone to read, including us. The one exception is the abandoned photos described above: deleting a room removes its database contents, and those few images are cleared by the photo game's own tidy-up rather than with the room.

Where the data is held

Our server and database are operated for us by Supabase and are hosted in the European Union (Ireland, eu-west-1). Supabase acts as our data processor. If you use the app from outside the European Union, this means the data described above is transferred to and processed in the EU.

Crash reporting

This app does not send crash reports or diagnostic data anywhere. It contains a crash-reporting library, but that library is not switched on: it needs a key to be supplied when the app is built, and no such key is configured, so it never starts and never transmits anything.

Advertising and analytics

There are none. The app contains no advertising software, no usage analytics, no attribution or install-tracking software, and no advertising identifier is read or transmitted. We do not build a profile of you, and there is nothing to opt out of.

Children

Knockabout is a party game for friends and is not directed at children. It contains an optional bolder set of prompts, switched off by default, which is intended for adults.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age, because the app has no accounts and asks for no personal details at all. If a child does play, the only thing that can reach our server is what any player sends during a game, and that is deleted with the room: within the hour for every game but Caught, and within eight hours for that one. If you are a parent or guardian and have a question about this, write to zazagdev@gmail.com.

Your rights

Because there is no account, there is no profile for us to look up, export or correct, and we cannot identify you from the data we hold. In practice:

  • To delete everything you have sent us: stop playing. The room and everything in it, yours included, is deleted about an hour after the room goes quiet, or about eight hours for a Caught room, which is built to be quiet for most of an evening. Leaving a game that is still running does not delete your part of it on its own, because the others are still playing the same room: what you sent goes when the room does. If you want it gone sooner, ask everybody to close the game, or write to us at the address below.
  • To delete everything held on your phone: clear your history from the Profile screen, or uninstall the app.
  • If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you have rights of access, correction, erasure, restriction and objection under data protection law. Because we hold no identifying information, we will usually be unable to link a request to any particular data, and the practical answer is the one above. You may still contact us at zazagdev@gmail.com, and you have the right to complain to your local supervisory authority.
